# L6 constructor evidence

The access-policy evaluator needs to know whether a selected constructor establishes its result. `check_record_constructor` takes the immutable Psi package **that defines the machine** and its exact declaration identity. The L5 producer test prepares and lowers the shipped toolchain as a verification unit. It checks that the constructor has a body and that the nine catalog-supplied declarations have their separate identities. [Producer test](../05_prepared-trees-to-terminal-psi/tests/obligation_library.rs)

For the supported straight-line constructor, the checker follows each field to a read through a shared formal, a transfer from an owned formal, or a zero of its declared type. It checks the returned nominal application, nested data requirements, ownership and every declared guarantee. The resulting `CheckedRecordConstructor` borrows the defining unit and identifies the exact inputs and result to which the proof applies. The mutation tests alter the unit supplied to the checker, including a nested requirement that rejects zero. [Checker](src/lib.rs) · [Storage rules](src/storage.rs) · [Checks against the real constructor](tests/constructor.rs)

The caller-side check reads the selected policy from its original package, including the domain, conformance member, constructor target, and borrow of the schema formal. It verifies the application against the constructor’s checked signature and retains the exact issuance case present at the call. Substituting a different package, a false guarantee, a different discriminant without its fields, or a borrow that ends too early invalidates the evidence. [Selected policy calls](tests/policy_call.rs)

Executing the policy still requires establishing its actual arguments, the readable lifetime of a shared argument, and admission of the selected execution. A validated access plan also requires checking the returned data against the requested schema, regions and callable identities. The constructor evidence alone grants no issuance authority.
