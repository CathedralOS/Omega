The arithmetic rules, call requirements, data invariants and ranking rules are all still enforced, and now each acceptance is a certificate the proof kernel accepted (`R6md:3-13`), where the old code answered with its own yes or no. About a fifth of the old rules apply to constructs the current compiler does not read at all: machines with more than one state, recursion that is not a tail call, `requires` written as a membership or a named proposition, quotient data, user-declared measures, value-to-address casts and mathematical `Nat` casts. Those programs are refused, so nothing is silently unenforced there, but every rule in that group has to be rebuilt when the construct is served.

The one rule I could find neither a check nor a refusal for is the first half of R66: a Boolean expression written as a statement used to be a proof obligation, and today it is evaluated and discarded (`S5/read/machine.rs:4449-4452`). The specification now says there is no assertion primitive (`wiki/spec/proofs/contracts.md:804-806`), so this may be a deliberate language change, but a program that relied on the old rule now compiles with that statement unproved. Trait laws (R54) have no code of their own in stages 03 to 06; the nearest check is the proof that an implementation's contract refines its requirement's.

Two current rules are stricter than the old ones. A disjunction in a clause no longer forms its right side under the denial of its left (`requires true || flags - 1 <= ...` is rejected, `S6/tests/contract_formation.rs:342-360`). And a recursive machine can no longer assume its own `ensures` anywhere: a tail self-call is a jump with no guarantee row, and any other self-call is refused (`S5/components.rs:169`). That removes three of the old code's suspected wrong accepts (self-citation with a unit result, self-induction without a measure check, `Nat` descent decided by the names `Nat` and `saturating_sub`); I found no test of those names in stages 05 or 06.
