## Five things a maintainer most needs to know
1. Wire format and identity are two encodings of the same content with separate numbering of versions: wire `VERSION = 51` (`encoding.rs:1986`), identity domain `v38` (`identity.rs:538`), module name `v6` (`encoding.rs:99`). Decode refuses any other wire version with `UnsupportedVersion`.
2. Decode is strict in three layers: literal tag tables with `InvalidField` for unknown tags, zero-id rejection, and finally a recomputed-identity comparison (`encoding.rs:215-218`). Tests prove single-field substitutions are caught (`program.rs` tests).
3. Tag tables are hand-written twice (encode in `identity.rs:132-242`, decode in `encoding.rs:390-695` and `:827-985`); the encoder is an exhaustive match and the decoder is not, so a new instruction kind compiles but is refused at decode until its tag is added.
4. The `allow_*` flags in decode are always true on the shipped path (`encoding.rs:192`, `:812`); they are leftovers of earlier versions, and their ordering in the match creates shadowed arms (kind-level and encoded-memory tag 19, `encoding.rs:1066` vs `:1076`).
5. `cleanup` effect is never serialized beyond a single 0 byte and always decoded as `NoneV1` (`encoding.rs:335`, `identity.rs:96`); ownership and cleanup row encoders exist here (`identity.rs:362-451`) and again in `target-vocabulary` (`structural.rs:272`).
