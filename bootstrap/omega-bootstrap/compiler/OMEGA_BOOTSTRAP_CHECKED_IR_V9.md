CKIR schema major 9 is the private successor for the selected remaining
primitive ordered-comparison relation. It adds opcode 19 `Greater` and opcode
20 `GreaterEqual` and otherwise inherits CKIR8's header, table order, row
widths, canonical ordering, meanings, resources, statuses, and publication
rules. Opcodes 15 through 18 remain available but are no longer required.
Earlier OMGLOW and CKIR identities and bytes remain frozen.

This is bounded bridge-cost evidence for exact unsigned `u8` and `u32` `>` and
`>=`. It is neither a public Omega IR nor Boolean, `u64`, coercing, aggregate,
sum, or user-defined ordering coverage, and it is not final admission to
`Ωself`.

## 1. Independent lowering and resolution versions

Compiler-owned primitive ordered syntax creates no declaration, type, field,
case, call target, or other resolution identity. There is therefore no
`OMGRSW4`. Resolution continues to publish the least canonical OMGRSW1, 2, or
3 required by the exact source, independently of `>` or `>=` syntax.

The resolved-source lowerer consumes the distinct `OMGLOWA` frame. `A` is the
single-byte successor label; the numeric header version remains 10:

```text
offset  width  field
0       8      magic: ASCII "OMGLOWA\0"
8       u16    schema major: 10
10      u16    schema minor: 0
12      u16    flags: zero
14      u16    header size: 32
16      u32    exact total frame length
20      u32    exact OMGCOMP length
24      u32    exact selected OMGRSW1/2/3 length
28      u32    exact selected OMGRSW major: 1, 2, or 3
32      ...    exact OMGCOMP || exact selected OMGRSW || exact EOF
```

The selector is framing, not authority. The lowerer validates the complete
selected witness and its canonical least-version relation to the source.
Component and aggregate ceilings remain 267,280, 524,288, and 791,600 bytes.

`OMGLOWA` requires at least one admitted `>` or `>=` and always emits CKIR9. A
source needing only inherited equality or logical forms retains its earlier
least lowering relation. OMGLOWA accepts inherited `!`, `&&`, `||`, and `==`
but rejects a source without an admitted new ordered token, a selector/witness
mismatch, and every old/new frame cross-pair.

## 2. Selected source relation

Omega `>` and `>=` compare two numeric operands in authored left/right order
and produce canonical `bool`. They occupy the existing ordering precedence,
above equality; equality remains above `&&`. Ordering operators associate left.
Consequently `3 > 2 == true && 2 >= 2` emits `Greater`, `ScalarEqual`,
`GreaterEqual`, then `LogicalAnd`. A chained `3 > 2 > 1` constructs the first
Boolean result as the second comparison's left operand and is rejected by this
numeric-only relation; it is not silently parsed as `3 > (2 > 1)`.

The admitted operand pair is deliberately narrow:

- both operands have the same primitive carrier, exact `u8` or exact `u32`;
- compatible constrained variants are admitted when their carrier kind is the
  same;
- an integer literal is contextualized by its typed numeric peer, or defaults
  to exact `u32` when both operands are integer literals; and
- both operands terminate, are pure, and cannot trap or cause an observable
  mutation, allocation, call, boundary, or user-defined dispatch.

Calls, array or slice indexing, trapping arithmetic, mutation, constructors,
Boolean operands, `u64`, mixed carriers, records, sums, coercion, and
user-defined ordering are rejected.

Canonical lowering emits one opcode-19 or opcode-20 row for every authored
admitted token, in source construction order. It does not swap operands into
`Less`/`LessEqual`, fold literals, widen values, or synthesize dispatch.

This selected slice emits no new transition bound fact for `>` or `>=`.
Specifically, it does not reuse the upper-bound refinement generated by the
existing admitted `<` and `<=` forms. Such a fact would be unsound for a lower-
bound predicate. Existing `<`/`<=` fact behavior is unchanged; no general fact
refinement from `>`/`>=` is claimed here.

## 3. Opcodes 19 and 20

Both operations use the inherited 40-byte operation row:

- schema major is exactly 9;
- result kind is value and result ID is the next dense value ID;
- result type is the exact canonical Boolean type;
- operand count is exactly two;
- operands are two visible values whose type rows have the same `u8` or `u32`
  carrier kind, in authored left/right order; and
- both immediate words and inherited flags/reserved fields are zero.

Opcode 19 produces one exactly when the unsigned left carrier value is greater
than the right. Opcode 20 produces one exactly when it is greater than or equal
to the right. CKIR9 requires at least one opcode-19 or opcode-20 row. CKIR8
rejects both opcodes, while CKIR9 may contain inherited opcode 18 without
requiring it. Changing only a CKIR8 major cannot create canonical CKIR9.

## 4. Resources, status, and non-expansion

Each ordered-comparison node consumes one expression-depth level, operation
row, two operand-vector words, value, and four-byte scalar slot. It introduces
no new table, arena, allocator, or ceiling. Inherited ceilings remain
normative, including expression depth 8, 32,768 operations, 94,208 operand
words, 36,864 values, the 262,144-byte machine frame, the 1-MiB text bound, and
the complete CKIR byte bound. Total expression depth 8 succeeds and depth 9
selects 252.

Malformed syntax, an excluded carrier or expression, chained-result type
failure, identity/type/visibility/arity/order drift, nonzero immediates or
reserved fields, a missing required new ordered operation, and version cross-
pairs select 251. Resource exhaustion selects 252. Neither status publishes
CKIR or ELF bytes.

  identity, pinned `SETA`/`SETAE` templates, and artifact mutations.

These gates establish the selected producer, independent CKIR meaning, and
conservative backend. Same-frame persisted-Beta refinement remains a separate
admission obligation. Nothing here admits excluded carriers or operands,
general ordered comparison, new transition facts, or the feature to final
`Ωself`.
