All arithmetic carriers and accumulator formats are binary64. Dot executes
product = left[i]*right[i], followed by sum = sum+product, in increasing index
order. Axpy executes product = alpha*x[i], then y[i] = product+y[i]. Self axpy
uses alpha*old+old, never (alpha+1)*old. No fused multiply-add, compensated sum,
implicit widening, hidden allocation or hidden copying. These are explicit
fixed-format kernels; they do not establish or replace NF02's policy surface.

Try false means unequal lengths or nonfinite input/intermediate/result.
Outputs are unchanged on false. Dot and norm publish only a final finite result.
Axpy validates every element before its write pass, under unchanged loans.
Empty dot/norm publish +0.0. Zero norm publishes +0.0. Empty axpy succeeds for
finite alpha. Nonfinite alpha rejects even an empty axpy.

Scaled norm maintains scale and sum of scaled squares, initialized to 0 and 1.
Each nonzero magnitude raises scale or adds a ratio square at most one.
The final scale*sqrt(squares) can overflow: finite inputs do not guarantee a
representable norm, and that result rejects. Small relative contributions may
underflow in ratios; this is not an exact norm or arbitrary precision API.
Dot is not overflow-safe merely because norm is; overflowing products reject.

Aliasing:
  dot may share its two immutable inputs, but scalar output must be disjoint.
  norm's scalar output must be disjoint from input.
  ordinary axpy permits mutation of existing y, with disjoint x and y loans.
  exact x=y is permitted through try_axpy_self_f64 and one exclusive loan.
  overlapping shared/exclusive loans, including partial overlap, reject.
  No runtime address comparison, implicit scratch copy, or allocation is used.
