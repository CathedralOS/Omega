All arithmetic carriers and accumulator formats are binary64. Dot executes
product = left[i]*right[i], then sum = sum+product in increasing index order.
AXPY executes product = alpha*x[i], then y[i] = product+y[i]. Self-AXPY uses
alpha*old+old, never (alpha+1)*old. No fused contraction, compensated sum,
implicit widening, hidden allocation or copying is permitted. These fixed-format
kernels do not establish or replace numeric_accuracy_policy.

False means unequal lengths or a nonfinite input/intermediate/result.
Outputs remain unchanged on false. Dot and norm publish only a final finite
result. AXPY validates every element before its write pass, under unchanged
loans. Empty dot/norm and zero norm publish +0.0. Empty AXPY succeeds for finite
alpha; nonfinite alpha rejects even an empty AXPY.

Scaled norm maintains scale and sum of scaled squares, initially 0 and 1.
Each nonzero magnitude raises scale or adds a ratio square at most one.
Final scale*sqrt(squares) can overflow: finite inputs do not guarantee a
representable norm, so that result rejects. Small contributions may underflow
in ratios. This is not an exact-real or arbitrary-precision norm. Dot is not
overflow-safe merely because norm is; overflowing products reject.

Aliasing:
  Dot may share immutable inputs; scalar output must be disjoint.
  Norm's scalar output must be disjoint from input.
  Ordinary AXPY requires disjoint shared x and exclusive y backing loans.
  Exact x=y uses try_axpy_self_f64 and one exclusive loan.
  Overlapping shared/exclusive loans, including partial overlap, reject.
  No runtime address comparison, scratch copy or allocation is used.
